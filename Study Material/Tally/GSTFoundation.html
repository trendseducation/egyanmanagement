1. GST in India

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is India's unified indirect tax, launched on July 1, 2017. It replaced a complex web of 17 different taxes — Central Excise, Service Tax, VAT, CST, Octroi, and more — with a single, destination-based tax on the supply of goods and services [citation:9].

Before GST, businesses dealt with different tax rates in every state, separate warehouses, and endless paperwork at state borders. GST created a single national market — goods move freely across India, and tax is collected at every stage with credit for taxes already paid [citation:1].

The numbers tell the story: registered taxpayers grew from 60 lakh to over 1.51 crore, and GST collection hit an all-time high of ₹22.08 lakh crore in 2024–25 [citation:1]. For students learning Tally, GST is not optional knowledge — it's the foundation of modern Indian accounting.

Why GST matters for Tally learners: Every sales invoice, purchase entry, and tax report in TallyPrime revolves around GST. Understanding how GST works in Tally is the single most valuable skill for any accounting student in India today.
The three faces of GST. Intra-state sales attract CGST + SGST (shared between Centre and State). Inter-state sales attract IGST (collected by Centre).

2. GST Foundation Concepts

Before opening TallyPrime, you need to understand five core GST concepts. These form the foundation for everything you'll configure in the software:

GSTIN

A 15-digit unique number assigned to every registered business. Format: 2 digits state code + 10 digits PAN + 1 entity code + 1 check digit + 1 default 'Z'. Every Tally company needs a valid GSTIN.

HSN Code

Harmonized System of Nomenclature — a classification code for goods. Required on invoices. 4-digit HSN for turnover up to ₹5 crore; 6-digit for above ₹5 crore. Manual entry no longer allowed — must select from dropdown [citation:3].

SAC Code

Services Accounting Code — the HSN equivalent for services. Required when billing for services like consulting, tuition, or freelancing. Used in service ledgers in Tally.

Tax Slabs

GST rates: 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% (plus cess on luxury items). Essential goods at 5%, standard at 18%, luxury at 28%. Rates are configured in Tally masters.

Input Tax Credit (ITC)

The GST you pay on purchases is creditable against GST collected on sales. ITC prevents tax-on-tax. In Tally, ITC is tracked automatically through Input CGST/SGST/IGST ledgers.

Composition Scheme

Small businesses (turnover up to ₹1.5 crore for goods) can opt for a simplified scheme with lower rates and less compliance [citation:1][citation:13]. Tally supports both Regular and Composition registration types.

Student tip: Remember the hierarchy — GSTIN identifies who you are. HSN/SAC identifies what you're selling. Tax slab determines how much tax applies. ITC determines what you can claim back. Master these four and Tally setup becomes logical, not confusing.

3. Setting Up GST in TallyPrime

GST is not enabled by default in a new Tally company. Here's the exact sequence to activate it:

Step 1: Enable GST at Company Level

Open company → Press F11 (Company Features) → Set "Enable Goods and Services Tax (GST)" to Yes
F11

Step 2: Enter GST Registration Details

State (auto-filled from company info), GSTIN/UIN (15 digits), Registration Type (Regular or Composition)
Enter
Periodicity of GSTR-1: Monthly or Quarterly (based on turnover — up to ₹5 crore can file quarterly)
Enter

Step 3: Set Up Connected GST (Optional but Powerful)

Enter GST Username (from GST portal) and choose Mode of Filing: DSC (Digital Signature) or EVC (Electronic Verification Code) [citation:10]
Ctrl+A
Common mistake: Entering a wrong GSTIN or selecting Composition when you're actually Regular. This causes return mismatches with the portal that take hours to fix. Double-check every detail before pressing Ctrl+A to save.
Three steps to GST-ready Tally. Enable at company level → enter registration details → configure filing preferences.

4. Ledgers & Masters for GST

GST calculation in Tally depends on three layers of configuration: tax ledgers, sales/purchase ledgers, and stock items. Each plays a specific role:

Tax Ledgers (under Duties & Taxes)

Input CGST

GST paid on purchases within the same state. This is your credit — claim it against Output CGST. Create under group Duties & Taxes → GST.

Input SGST

State GST paid on purchases within the same state. Claim against Output SGST. Separate ledger from Input CGST.

Input IGST

Integrated GST paid on inter-state purchases. Claim against Output IGST or adjust against CGST/SGST in order.

Output CGST / SGST / IGST

GST collected on sales. These are your liabilities. The difference between Output and Input tax is what you pay to the government.

Stock Items & Sales Ledgers

In each Stock Item: set GST applicability to Applicable, enter HSN code, and specify the GST rate (e.g., 18%) [citation:6]
Alt+C
In Sales Ledger: set GST applicability to Applicable, specify tax rate (or inherit from stock item)
Enter
Configure Source of GST Rate: Ledger-first (default) or Stock Item-first, depending on your business practice [citation:2]
F12
HSN validation: As of January 2025, HSN codes must be selected from the portal's dropdown in TallyPrime — manual entry is no longer permitted for compliance. Always validate your HSN codes against the GST portal's list [citation:3].

5. Recording GST Transactions

Once masters are set up, recording GST invoices is straightforward. Tally calculates tax automatically based on the party's state and the stock item's rate:

Purchase (F9)

Press F9 → Select supplier → Enter stock items → Tally auto-calculates Input CGST/SGST (same state) or Input IGST (different state)
F9
If using Receipt Note (Alt+F9), link the tracking number in the purchase invoice to clear the "Purchase Bills Pending" ledger [citation:3]
Alt+F9

Sales (F8)

Press F8 → Select customer → Enter items → Tally auto-calculates Output CGST/SGST or Output IGST
F8
Enter the customer's GSTIN — Tally can fetch and validate it against the portal for accuracy [citation:7]
Enter
For applicable businesses, generate e-Invoice and e-Way Bill directly from the sales voucher
Alt+E
The beauty of integrated accounting: One sales entry in TallyPrime simultaneously updates your stock, your accounting ledgers, your GST liability, and your GSTR-1 data. No separate data entry for returns [citation:3].

6. GST Returns & Filing

TallyPrime doesn't just record transactions — it prepares your GST returns automatically from the data you've already entered. The two key returns for every regular taxpayer:

GSTR-1

Details of all outward supplies (sales) — invoice-wise. Buyers use this to claim ITC. Filed monthly or quarterly (QRMP). Tally groups your invoices into B2B, B2C, exports automatically.

GSTR-3B

Summary return showing total tax liability, ITC claimed, and net tax payable. Filed monthly (or quarterly with monthly payments). This is where you actually pay the government.

Before Filing: Check Uncertain Transactions

Open GSTR-1 report → Check Uncertain Transactions list. Missing GSTIN, blank HSN, or wrong tax rates appear here. Fix all before filing [citation:12]
Alt+G

Direct Filing from TallyPrime

Generate GSTR-1 → Review → Press Alt+E to export JSON, or use Connected GST to upload directly
Alt+E
File using EVC (OTP on mobile) or DSC — no need to log in to GST portal separately [citation:4]
Alt+S
Go to Alt+G → GSTR-3B → Review liability → File the same way
Alt+G
Best practice: File GSTR-1 first so your buyers get their ITC on time, then file GSTR-3B to settle your own liability. Filing in reverse order causes mismatches and delayed credits [citation:4].
