1. What is a Ledger?

In TallyPrime, a ledger is the fundamental accounting unit — it represents a single account under which all transactions are recorded. Every sale, purchase, payment, receipt, and tax entry ultimately posts to one or more ledgers .

Think of ledgers as individual buckets. "Cash" is a ledger. "Sales" is a ledger. "Output CGST" is a ledger. Each bucket tracks the balance of a specific account. When you record a transaction, Tally debits one ledger and credits another — the double-entry system in action.

Without properly configured ledgers, Tally cannot compute GST, generate reports, or produce a trial balance. Ledger configuration is the foundation on which all accounting rests — and it's where most beginners make costly mistakes.

Ledger vs. Group: A group is a category (like "Assets" or "Liabilities"). A ledger is a specific account within a group (like "Cash" or "Bank Loan"). Groups determine how ledgers appear in the balance sheet and profit & loss statement. Every ledger must belong to exactly one group .
Everything flows through ledgers. Groups organize ledgers. Ledgers record transactions. Transactions build your financial statements.

The #1 beginner mistake: Choosing the wrong group. Putting a bank loan under "Current Assets" instead of "Current Liabilities" distorts your balance sheet. Always double-check the group — it's the single most important field in ledger creation.

Bank Ledger

Group: Bank Accounts. Enter account number, IFSC, and branch details. This enables banking features like reconciliation and e-payments.

Cash Ledger

Group: Cash-in-Hand. Only one cash ledger is recommended. Tally creates "Cash" by default when you enable cash transactions.

Sundry Debtors (Customers)

Group: Sundry Debtors. One ledger per customer. Enable "Maintain balances bill-by-bill" to track outstanding invoices per customer.

Sundry Creditors (Suppliers)

Group: Sundry Creditors. One ledger per supplier. Critical for GST ITC tracking — each supplier's GSTIN should be recorded.

Sales Ledger

Group: Sales Accounts. Set GST applicability to Applicable and specify tax rate. Use separate ledgers for different GST slabs if needed.

Purchase Ledger

Group: Purchase Accounts. Enable GST and set rate. This is what generates Input Tax Credit entries automatically.

3. Ledger Groups

Groups are the skeleton of your chart of accounts. TallyPrime comes with 28 pre-defined groups, each mapped to a primary group (Assets, Liabilities, Income, Expenses). Understanding the hierarchy prevents months of confusion:

The chart of accounts hierarchy. Primary groups determine financial statement placement. Sub-groups organize related ledgers. Ledgers hold transactions.
Pro tip: Use Alt+C to create a ledger "on the fly" from any voucher. Tally prompts you to select the group instantly — saving time when you're mid-transaction and realize a ledger doesn't exist yet.

4. Tax Configuration

GST tax configuration in TallyPrime happens at three levels. Missing any one causes calculation errors or return mismatches:

Company Level

Enable GST in F11 → Company Features. Enter GSTIN, state, registration type (Regular/Composition), and filing periodicity. This activates the entire GST module.

Ledger Level

For sales/purchase ledgers: set GST applicability, type of supply (Goods/Services), and default tax rate. This determines the tax applied to each transaction.

Stock Item Level

For goods: enter HSN code and GST rate. If stock item rate differs from ledger rate, Tally uses the Source of GST Rate setting (default: Ledger-first).

Key Tax Configuration Settings

Source of GST Rate: Ledger-first (default) or Stock Item-first. Determines which rate takes priority when both are set
F12
Registration Type: Regular (full ITC, monthly returns) or Composition (lower rate, no ITC, quarterly)
F11
HSN/SAC Code: Mandatory for GST invoices. Select from validated dropdown (manual entry not permitted since Jan 2025)
Alt+C
GST Rate: 0%, 0.25%, 3%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% — set per stock item or per ledger
Enter
Reverse Charge: Set "Applicable" for RCM transactions (e.g., purchases from unregistered dealers, certain services)
Enter
Periodicity of GSTR-1: Monthly or Quarterly. Businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore can opt for quarterly (QRMP scheme)
F11
HSN compliance alert: Since January 2025, manual HSN entry is no longer permitted in TallyPrime. All HSN codes must be selected from the portal-validated dropdown . If you're learning on an older version, upgrade to the latest release to stay compliant.
Best practice: Create separate sales ledgers for different GST rates — "Local Sales 18%", "Local Sales 5%", "Interstate Sales 18%". This makes GSTR-1 generation automatic and error-free. Mixing rates in one ledger forces manual tax overrides.

5. GST Ledgers

GST ledgers live under the group Duties & Taxes. TallyPrime auto-creates some of them when you enable GST, but understanding each is critical:

Input CGST — Central GST paid on intra-state purchases. Your credit against Output CGST
Duties & Taxes
Input SGST — State GST paid on intra-state purchases. Credit against Output SGST
Duties & Taxes
Input IGST — Integrated GST paid on inter-state purchases. Credit usable against any output tax
Duties & Taxes
Output CGST / SGST / IGST — Tax collected on sales. These are your liabilities
Duties & Taxes
Round Off — Auto-created. Absorbs paise differences in invoices. Never delete this ledger
Duties & Taxes
Auto-creation: When you enable GST and create your first sales/purchase transaction, TallyPrime automatically creates the required tax ledgers (CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess). You don't need to create them manually — but you should verify they exist under Duties & Taxes.

ITC Set-off Order

Step 1: IGST credit → set off against IGST, then CGST, then SGST
1
Step 2: CGST credit → set off against CGST, then IGST (not SGST)
2
Step 3: SGST credit → set off against SGST, then IGST (not CGST)
3
Memory trick: "IGST is the flexible one" — it can offset any tax. CGST and SGST are rigid — each only offsets its own type and IGST. TallyPrime handles this order automatically in GSTR-3B, but understanding it helps you spot errors.

7. Tips & Best Practices

These practices separate clean accounting from chaotic books. Learn them early and save yourself months of debugging:

Naming Convention

Use consistent names: HDFC Bank Current A/c, not just HDFC. For customers: Rajesh Kumar (Delhi) to distinguish duplicates. Good names = fast search = fewer errors.

Verify Group Placement

Before saving any ledger, ask: "Does this belong in Assets, Liabilities, Income, or Expenses?" A misplaced group silently corrupts your balance sheet and P&L.

Separate GST-Rate Ledgers

Create Local Sales 18%, Local Sales 5%, Interstate Sales 18%. This makes GST reports automatic. One mixed-rate ledger forces manual tax entry on every invoice.

Enable Bill-by-Bill

For Sundry Debtors and Creditors, enable "Maintain balances bill-by-bill". This tracks outstanding invoices per party — essential for collections and GST reconciliation.

Complete Bank Details

For bank ledgers: enter account number, IFSC, branch, and opening balance. This enables bank reconciliation (BRS) and e-payment features in TallyPrime.

Record Party GSTIN

For B2B customers and suppliers, always record their GSTIN in the ledger or party details. Without it, GSTR-1 cannot classify the sale as B2B — your buyer loses ITC.

Use Display → Ledger

After setup, go to Display More Reports → Account Books → Ledger and select each ledger. Verify the group, opening balance, and GST details are correct before entering transactions.

Never Delete Auto-Ledgers

Tally auto-creates ledgers like Round Off, Profit & Loss A/c, and GST ledgers. Never delete these — they're required for reports and will cause errors.

Critical check: After creating all ledgers, run Alt+L (Trial Balance) and Alt+B (Balance Sheet). If totals don't match, a ledger is likely in the wrong group or has a wrong opening balance. Fix this before recording any transactions — correcting later is far harder.
Practice plan: Day 1 — create 10 ledgers covering all four primary groups. Day 2 — explore F11 and F12 settings to see what's configurable. Day 3 — create GST tax ledgers and verify auto-creation. Day 4 — set up a customer with GSTIN and bill-by-bill. Day 5 — run trial balance and verify every ledger appears in the correct place.
