1. What is a Purchase?

A purchase in TallyPrime is a transaction where your business buys goods or services from a supplier (vendor). It is the mirror image of a sale — money flows out, goods or services flow in, and a supplier invoice documents the exchange .

Every purchase triggers four simultaneous updates in Tally: (1) stock quantity increases, (2) purchase expense is recorded, (3) supplier's payable balance increases, and (4) if GST applies, Input Tax Credit (ITC) is created. One entry, four impacts — this is double-entry accounting in action.

For a business, purchases are the largest expense category. Recording them correctly is essential for accurate stock valuation, GST compliance, and profit calculation. A single mis-keyed purchase invoice can throw off your balance sheet for the entire year.

Purchase vs. Expense: A purchase is buying goods for resale or production (tracked in stock). An expense is a cost that doesn't create stock — like rent, electricity, or salary. Tally treats them differently: purchases affect stock and COGS; expenses hit the P&L directly.
The purchase flow. Goods arrive from the supplier, you record the invoice in Tally, and the software updates stock, ITC, and your payable to the supplier.

2. Types of Purchases

TallyPrime records purchases in several voucher types, each designed for a specific real-world scenario. Choosing the right one keeps your books accurate:

Purchase Voucher (F9)

The standard entry. Goods received + invoice received simultaneously. Stock increases, supplier payable increases, ITC is recorded. Used for most routine purchases.

Receipt Note (Alt+F9)

Goods arrived but invoice not yet received. Stock increases, but supplier payable does NOT. Tracked in "Purchase Bills Pending" liability. Used when goods arrive before the bill.

Purchase Invoice (F9)

Complete purchase — goods + invoice together. If you used a Receipt Note earlier, link the tracking number to clear "Purchase Bills Pending".

Purchase Return (Ctrl+F5)

Goods returned to supplier (damaged, wrong, excess). Stock decreases, supplier payable reduces, ITC reverses. Also called "Rejection Out".

Debit Note

Used when supplier's invoice has an error (wrong rate, extra quantity). Reduces supplier payable. Often used alongside purchase returns for GST compliance.

Import Purchase

Goods from outside India. Attracts IGST + customs duty. Uses a different ledger structure with import duties. Recorded as "Interstate" supply for GST.

Which voucher to use? If goods and invoice arrive together → F9 Purchase Voucher. If goods arrive first → Alt+F9 Receipt Note, then later link in Purchase Invoice. If you return goods → Ctrl+F5 Purchase Return. Simple rule: match the voucher to the real-world event.

4. GST in Purchases

When you buy goods or services, GST is charged by the supplier on top of the base price. The type of GST depends on where the supplier is located:

Intra-State Purchase

Supplier in the same state as you. GST splits into CGST + SGST (each at half the rate). E.g., 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST. Both are creditable as ITC.

Inter-State Purchase

Supplier in a different state. GST charged as IGST (full rate). E.g., 18% IGST. Creditable against any output tax (CGST, SGST, or IGST).

Import Purchase

Goods from outside India. Attracts IGST + Basic Customs Duty (BCD). IGST is creditable; BCD is not. Requires separate import ledgers in Tally.

Reverse Charge (RCM)

For certain purchases (unregistered dealers, specific services), you pay GST to the government instead of the supplier. Both output and input entries are recorded; net effect is ITC.

Composition Supplier

A supplier under the Composition Scheme does not charge GST. No ITC available to you. Recorded as purchase with 0% GST. Common with small vendors.

Exempt / Nil-Rated

Goods like fresh produce, milk, or certain medicines are GST-exempt. No tax charged, no ITC. Recorded in Tally with 0% or "Exempt" applicability.

Invoice check: If supplier is in same state → CGST + SGST should appear on the bill. If different state → only IGST. If both appear, the invoice is wrong — question it before recording. Wrong splits are the #1 cause of ITC mismatch.

5. Input Tax Credit (ITC)

Input Tax Credit is the GST you paid on purchases that you can deduct from the GST you collect on sales. It's what makes GST a tax on value added, not on the entire sale price .

Example: You buy goods for ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST (18%). You sell them for ₹15,000 + ₹2,700 GST. Without ITC, you'd pay ₹2,700 to the government — tax on the full ₹15,000. With ITC, you pay only ₹900 (₹2,700 output − ₹1,800 input) — tax only on the ₹5,000 value you added.

ITC reversal: If you return goods to the supplier, or the supplier issues a credit note, or you don't pay within 180 days — the ITC must be reversed (added back to your liability). Tally handles this automatically when you record a Purchase Return.
Reconciliation habit: Every month, match Tally's purchase register with GSTR-2B on the GST portal. Discrepancies mean ITC risk — either your supplier didn't file, or an invoice number doesn't match. Fix these before filing GSTR-3B.

7. Tips & Best Practices

Good purchase recording habits save hours of reconciliation pain later. Follow these practices from day one:

Always Enter Invoice Number

Match the exact supplier invoice number. This is what the GST portal cross-checks. A typo can invalidate your ITC claim and cause a demand notice.

Verify GST Split

Same-state supplier → CGST + SGST. Different-state → only IGST. Never accept an invoice with the wrong split — question the supplier before recording.

Link Tracking Numbers

If you used a Receipt Note (Alt+F9), always link the tracking number in the subsequent Purchase Invoice. Otherwise "Purchase Bills Pending" stays uncleared forever.

Maintain Invoice Copies

Keep scanned PDFs of every purchase invoice. GST law requires you to produce them during audit (up to 6 years). Tally attaches them via Attach Documents feature.

Pay Within 180 Days

If you don't pay a supplier within 180 days, ITC reverses — you owe the government even though you haven't been paid by your customer. Track aging carefully.

Reconcile GSTR-2B Monthly

Download GSTR-2B from the GST portal and reconcile with Tally's purchase register. Mismatches mean ITC risk — fix before filing GSTR-3B, not after.

Use Separate Purchase Ledgers

Create Local Purchase 18%, Local Purchase 5%, Interstate Purchase 18%. Makes GST reports automatic. Mixed-rate ledgers force manual tax overrides every time.

Never Delete Old Purchases

If a purchase is wrong, reverse it with a Purchase Return or Debit Note. Never delete — GST law requires audit trails, and Tally keeps history that the portal may cross-check.

Critical check: After entering a batch of purchases, run Alt+L (Trial Balance) and verify that Purchase Accounts and Input CGST/SGST/IGST balances match your manual total. A mismatch means an entry was missed or a wrong tax rate was applied.
Practice plan: Day 1 — record 5 intra-state purchases. Day 2 — record 3 inter-state purchases (IGST). Day 3 — enter a Receipt Note, then link the invoice next day. Day 4 — record a Purchase Return and verify ITC reversal. Day 5 — reconcile your Purchase Register against GSTR-2B simulated data.
