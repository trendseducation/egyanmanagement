Returns & Adjustments

Returns are normal — damaged goods, wrong deliveries, excess stock. Master sales returns, purchase returns, credit notes, and debit notes in TallyPrime.

1. Why Returns Happen

A return in TallyPrime is a reversal of an earlier sale or purchase. Goods go back to the supplier (purchase return) or come back from the customer (sales return). Money moves the opposite way.

Returns happen for many normal reasons: damaged goods, wrong items delivered, excess quantity shipped, quality issues, or customer change of mind. A business without returns would be unusual — in fact, a healthy return rate of 2–8% is normal for retail.

But returns must be recorded correctly, not just deleted. Deleting an invoice erases the audit trail, breaks GST reporting, and can trigger penalties. Tally provides dedicated vouchers — Sales Return (Ctrl+F6) and Purchase Return (Ctrl+F5) — to reverse transactions cleanly while keeping history intact.

Never delete — always reverse. GST law requires every transaction (including corrections) to be traceable. A Sales Return creates a proper paper trail: original invoice linked, goods received back, credit note issued, GST reversed. A deleted invoice creates nothing but a future audit problem.
One return, three effects. Recording a sales return updates stock, GST liability, and customer receivable — simultaneously and correctly.

2. Types of Adjustments

TallyPrime handles six types of adjustments. Understanding each ensures you use the right voucher:

Sales Return (Ctrl+F6)

Customer returns goods. Stock increases, receivable decreases, Output GST reverses. Also called Rejection In. Voucher: Credit Note.

Purchase Return (Ctrl+F5)

You return goods to supplier. Stock decreases, payable decreases, Input GST reverses. Also called Rejection Out. Voucher: Debit Note.

Credit Note (Issued)

Issued to a customer reducing their payable. Used for sales returns, discounts, or invoice corrections. Reduces your Output GST liability.

Debit Note (Received)

Received from a supplier reducing your payable. Used for purchase returns, rate corrections, or quantity errors. Reduces your Input GST credit.

Rate Difference

Supplier or customer adjusts the rate after invoicing. Handled via a supplementary credit/debit note. Requires updating the original invoice reference.

Discount / Allowance

Post-invoice discount given to a customer. Usually recorded as a credit note. Post-sale trade discounts must be GST-adjusted.

Quick decision: Goods came back to you from a customer → Ctrl+F6 Sales Return. Goods went back to supplier → Ctrl+F5 Purchase Return. Invoice has an error (no goods movement) → Credit Note or Debit Note. Three simple rules, no confusion.

ITC reversal alert: Returning goods to a supplier means you lose the Input Tax Credit on those goods. This shows up in your GSTR-3B as a reversal. If you had already claimed the ITC, you must now reverse it — Tally handles this automatically, but reconcile GSTR-2B carefully.
Debit Note advantage: If the return is due to a quality issue or rate error, issue a Debit Note to the supplier instead of reversing the full invoice. This preserves the original invoice for audit purposes and clearly documents the adjustment.

5. Credit & Debit Notes

Credit notes and debit notes are formal adjustment documents used when the original invoice needs to be corrected — without reversing the entire transaction:

Credit Note (Issued)

Issued to a customer. Reduces their payable. Used for sales returns, discounts, or invoice corrections. Reduces your Output GST. Report in GSTR-1.

Debit Note (Received)

Received from a supplier. Reduces your payable. Used for purchase returns, rate differences, or quantity errors. Reduces your Input GST credit.

Rate Difference Adjustment

If the supplier or customer bills at a wrong rate, issue a supplementary credit/debit note for the difference. The original invoice stays intact.

Post-Sale Discount

Discounts given after the invoice must be GST-adjusted via credit note. The ITC benefit flows back to the customer. Not allowed for cash discounts unless pre-agreed.

Time Limits

Credit notes must be issued before the earlier of: 30 September of the following FY, or the annual return filing date. Late credit notes are not accepted.

GSTR Reporting

Credit notes appear in GSTR-1 Table 9B (B2B credit notes). Debit notes from suppliers appear in GSTR-2B. Both must match the original invoice reference.

Credit Note vs. Sales Return: A Sales Return is a stock movement — physical goods come back. A Credit Note is a financial adjustment — money owed reduces. Often they go together, but not always. A damaged good might be returned (Sales Return) and a credit note issued. A discount might warrant only a Credit Note.
Best practice: Always reference the original invoice number and date on every credit or debit note. Without it, the adjustment cannot be matched in GST returns, and the customer's ITC claim gets rejected by the portal.

7. Tips & Best Practices

Good returns handling protects your GST compliance and preserves customer trust. Follow these practices:

Always Link Original Invoice

Every credit or debit note must reference the original invoice number and date. Without this, GST portal rejects the adjustment and customer loses ITC.

Issue Within Time Limit

Credit notes must be issued before 30 September of the following FY or the annual return, whichever is earlier. Late notes are not accepted.

Record the Reason

Every return should state why: Damaged, Wrong item, Excess quantity, Rate difference. Helps in supplier claims and trend analysis.

Verify GST Reversal

After saving a return, open the linked GSTR-1 and verify the credit note appears in Table 9B. If not, your Output GST liability is overstated.

Reconcile GSTR-2B

For purchase returns, verify the debit note appears in GSTR-2B from the supplier's GSTR-1. Mismatch means your ITC reversal may not be accepted.

Update Physical Stock

Returns increase or decrease physical stock. After recording, verify stock quantities in Tally match your godown. Discrepancies mean unrecorded returns.

Never Delete Original Invoices

If an invoice is wrong, use a Credit Note or Debit Note — never delete. GST law requires every invoice to remain traceable, even if fully reversed.

Track Return Rate

Calculate monthly: (Returns ÷ Sales) × 100. Above 8% signals a quality or dispatch problem. Tally's Sales Register shows both, making analysis easy.

Critical check: After recording returns, run Alt+L (Trial Balance) and verify that Sales Returns and Purchase Returns balances are correctly reduced. Then verify GST reports show corresponding reversals. If not, the return was recorded but not properly linked to GST.
Practice plan: Day 1 — record 3 sales returns against existing invoices (full, partial, damaged). Day 2 — record 2 purchase returns and verify ITC reversal. Day 3 — issue a credit note for a post-sale discount. Day 4 — receive a debit note from a supplier for a rate error. Day 5 — reconcile returns against GSTR-1 and GSTR-2B, fixing any mismatches.
