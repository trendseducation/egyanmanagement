ITC & IMS Management

Input Tax Credit is the lifeblood of GST. Master the Invoice Management System to accept, reject, or hold supplier invoices — and claim every rupee you're entitled to.

1. What is ITC?

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is the GST you pay on purchases that you can deduct from the GST you collect on sales. It's the mechanism that makes GST a tax on value added, not on the entire sale price [citation:1].

Without ITC, a product would be taxed at every stage of the supply chain — cascading tax on tax. With ITC, the tax chain flows smoothly: each business pays only the difference between output tax and input tax. ITC is often called the "lifeblood" of GST for this reason [citation:1].

But ITC is not automatic. You can only claim it if you meet strict conditions: the supplier must have filed their return, the invoice must appear in your GSTR-2B, you must have received the goods, and you must pay your supplier within 180 days. This is where the Invoice Management System (IMS) comes in — it gives you control over which invoices enter your ITC pool [citation:7][citation:15].

The ITC formula: Net GST Payable = Output Tax on Sales − Input Tax Credit on Purchases. If you sold goods with ₹2,700 GST and purchased with ₹1,800 GST, you pay only ₹900 to the government. The remaining ₹1,800 is your ITC [citation:1].
How ITC works. Input tax offsets output tax. You pay only the difference — tax on the value you added, not the full sale price.

2. Invoice Management System (IMS)

The Invoice Management System (IMS) is a GST portal facility that gives you real-time control over your ITC. It sits between your supplier's return filing and your GSTR-2B, letting you review every invoice before it affects your tax return [citation:7][citation:15].

Before IMS, invoices flowed automatically into GSTR-2B — including wrong ones. If a supplier uploaded an invoice with your GSTIN by mistake, you'd have to deal with it during reconciliation. IMS changes that: you decide whether an invoice is accepted, rejected, or held pending, and your GSTR-2B reflects your decision [citation:12].

IMS became mandatory from April 1, 2026. Since then, ITC claims are hard-blocked for invoices that don't reflect as accepted in GSTR-2B. Ignoring IMS is no longer an option — it's the gatekeeper for your credit [citation:15].

Real-Time Visibility

See supplier invoices as soon as they're uploaded to GSTR-1 or IFF. No need to wait for GSTR-2B on the 14th. Act early, avoid surprises [citation:7].

You Control ITC

Accept correct invoices, reject wrong ones, hold pending ones for later review. Your GSTR-2B reflects your choices, not your supplier's errors [citation:4].

Pending Gives Time

Unsure about an invoice? Mark it Pending. It stays in IMS for future action without affecting current ITC. Perfect for goods not yet received [citation:8].

Recompute After 14th

GSTR-2B generates on the 14th. If you act after that, click "Recompute GSTR-2B" before filing GSTR-3B. Otherwise your changes won't reflect [citation:7][citation:11].

Deemed acceptance is risky: If you don't act on an invoice, it's auto-accepted when GSTR-2B generates. If that invoice is wrong — wrong GSTIN, wrong amount, or fake — you've just claimed ineligible ITC. The onus is on you to review [citation:7][citation:15].

3. Accept, Reject, Pending

Every invoice in IMS can be assigned one of three actions. Each has a specific impact on your GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B [citation:8][citation:12]:

Accept — The invoice is correct and eligible. ITC flows into GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B. You can reduce the ITC amount while accepting if partially eligible [citation:4].
Accept
Reject — The invoice is wrong, not yours, or fake. ITC is excluded from GSTR-2B and appears in the "ITC Rejected" section. You can also exclude it from GSTR-3B [citation:4].
Reject
Pending — Hold for later. No ITC impact this period. The invoice stays in IMS until you act or the Section 16(4) time limit expires [citation:8].
Pending
No Action — Default status. Treated as deemed accepted when GSTR-2B generates. Risky if the invoice is wrong [citation:7].
Auto
Pending is powerful: Use Pending when goods haven't arrived, or when you're waiting for supplier clarification. It protects you from claiming ITC prematurely, while keeping the invoice visible for future action. Just remember — pending invoices must eventually be accepted or rejected before the Section 16(4) deadline [citation:8][citation:12].
Pending is not allowed for: Original credit notes, upward amendments of credit notes, downward amendments where the original was rejected, and downward amendments of accepted invoices after GSTR-3B is filed. In these cases, you must Accept or Reject [citation:8][citation:12].

5. GSTR-2B Reconciliation

IMS and GSTR-2B work together. GSTR-2B is the static, legally binding statement of your ITC for the month. It's generated on the 14th, based on supplier filings and your IMS actions [citation:14].

Reconciliation is the process of matching GSTR-2B against your purchase records in Tally. Mismatches mean potential ITC loss — and the only way to fix them is to reconcile before filing GSTR-3B [citation:14].

Reconciliation in TallyPrime

Download GSTR-2B JSON from GST portal → Returns Dashboard → GSTR-2B → Download
Portal
In TallyPrime: Alt+G → GST Reports → GSTR-2B → press Alt+O to Import JSON
Alt+O
Tally sorts records: Reconciled (matched), Mismatched (amount/date/rate differs), Unreconciled (missing)
View
For Reconciled items: select all, mark Accepted — bulk action saves time
F10
For Mismatched items: contact supplier to correct their GSTR-1, or correct your purchase voucher
Follow up
Timing trap: If your supplier files GSTR-1 after the 11th, their invoice won't appear in this month's GSTR-2B. It rolls to next month. You must defer your ITC claim — claiming it now creates a mismatch and potential reversal [citation:14].
Round-off rule: Small mismatches under ₹10 are usually supplier rounding. Configure a Round Off ledger in Tally to absorb these automatically. Don't let ₹2 differences block your entire reconciliation [citation:14].

7. Tips & Best Practices

These practices protect your ITC and prevent reversals:

Act Before the 14th

GSTR-2B generates on the 14th. If you take action after that, you must click "Recompute GSTR-2B" before filing GSTR-3B. Act early to avoid last-minute work [citation:7][citation:11].

Never Ignore "No Action"

No action = deemed acceptance. If the invoice is wrong, you've just claimed ineligible ITC. Review every invoice, even if you think it's correct [citation:7].

Use Pending Wisely

Hold invoices when goods are in transit or clarification is pending. Pending gives you time without affecting ITC. But don't let them expire — act before the Section 16(4) deadline [citation:8].

Import GSTR-2B JSON Monthly

Download and import GSTR-2B into Tally every month. Reconcile line-by-line. Don't rely on portal views alone — Tally gives you the full picture [citation:14].

Follow Up on Mismatches

If an invoice is in GSTR-2B but not in your books (or vice versa), contact the supplier. They need to correct their GSTR-1. You can't fix their filing [citation:14].

Reject Wrong GSTIN Invoices

If a supplier uploaded an invoice with your GSTIN by mistake, reject it immediately. Accepting means claiming ITC you're not entitled to — a compliance risk [citation:4].

Track ITC Ledger

Monitor your Input CGST/SGST/IGST ledgers in Tally. The balance should match the accepted ITC in GSTR-2B. Discrepancies mean unrecorded or misrecorded purchases.

Never Delete — Reject or Reverse

If a purchase is wrong, don't delete the voucher. Reject the invoice in IMS, or record a purchase return. Deleting breaks the audit trail and can't be undone [citation:4].

Critical check: Before filing GSTR-3B, open the IMS dashboard and verify that every invoice you intend to claim ITC on shows "Accepted". Then verify that the ITC amount in GSTR-3B matches the accepted invoices in GSTR-2B. A single mismatch can trigger a notice.
Practice plan: Day 1 — open IMS and review 20 invoices. Accept matched, mark one Pending. Day 2 — import GSTR-2B and reconcile with your purchase register. Day 3 — find a mismatch and fix the purchase voucher. Day 4 — reject a wrong-GSTIN invoice and observe the ITC exclusion. Day 5 — verify final ITC in GSTR-3B matches your accepted list.
