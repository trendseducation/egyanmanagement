1. What is the Internet?

The Internet is a global network of interconnected computers that communicate using standard protocols. It allows billions of devices — phones, laptops, servers, smart TVs, even cars — to exchange information instantly across the world.

Think of it as a massive highway system for data. Information is broken into tiny packets, sent across thousands of routes, and reassembled at the destination — all in fractions of a second. It's the infrastructure behind the World Wide Web, email, video calls, online banking, and social media.

The Internet was born in 1969 as ARPANET (a US military research project). It went public in 1991 with the invention of the World Wide Web by Tim Berners-Lee. Today over 5.4 billion people are online.

Did you know? The Internet and the World Wide Web are not the same thing! The Internet is the network (the roads). The Web is just one service that runs on it (one type of traffic). Email, WhatsApp, video calls — they all use the Internet but aren't "the Web."
Billions of devices, one network. Laptops, phones, desktops, and servers all connect to the Internet — often through a router — and can talk to each other instantly.

2. How the Internet Works

Behind every web page, video, and email are several technologies working invisibly. Here are the core concepts you should know:

IP Address

Every device has a unique Internet Protocol address — like a postal address. IPv4 looks like 192.168.1.1; IPv6 is longer. Without IP addresses, data wouldn't know where to go.

DNS — Domain Name System

The Internet's "phonebook." Converts human-friendly names (google.com) into IP addresses. Without DNS, you'd have to memorize numbers for every website.

URL

Uniform Resource Locator — a website's full address. Structure: https://www.example.com/page. It tells the browser where to find a resource.

HTTP & HTTPS

HyperText Transfer Protocol — rules for transferring web pages. HTTPS (with S) is encrypted and secure. Always prefer HTTPS — look for the lock icon.

Packets

Data is broken into small packets, each sent independently across the network, then reassembled at the destination. This is why the Internet is so resilient.

Servers & Clients

Servers store websites and apps. Clients (your browser) request pages from servers. All web traffic follows this model.

ISP

Internet Service Provider — the company you pay for connectivity (Jio, Airtel, BSNL, etc.). They route your traffic to the wider Internet.

Routers

Direct packets between networks. Your home Wi-Fi router connects your devices to your ISP, which connects to the global backbone.

SSL/TLS Certificates

The "lock" icon in your browser. Certificates encrypt data between your device and the website, preventing eavesdropping on passwords and payments.

HTML, CSS, JavaScript

The three languages every web page is built from. HTML = structure, CSS = styling, JavaScript = interactivity.

🔒 https://www.example.com/page

https:// = secure protocol  ·  www.example.com = domain name  ·  /page = specific resource

Quick check: When you type google.com in the browser, the DNS resolves it to an IP. The browser connects via HTTPS to Google's server. The server sends HTML/CSS/JavaScript. Your browser renders the page. All this in under 1 second.

3. Browsers & Search Engines

A web browser is the app you use to access the Internet. A search engine is a website that helps you find information on the web. They're different things:

Google Chrome

World's most popular browser. Fast, syncs with Google account, huge extension library. Uses Blink engine.

Microsoft Edge

Microsoft's modern browser. Chromium-based (same engine as Chrome). Built-in AI, works great with Windows.

Mozilla Firefox

Open-source browser. Strong privacy focus, no Google tracking. Uses Gecko engine. Loved by developers.

Apple Safari

Default browser on Mac, iPhone, iPad. Fastest on Apple devices. Uses WebKit engine. Excellent battery life.

Opera

Chromium-based. Built-in VPN, ad-blocker, and messenger shortcuts. Popular in some countries.

Google Search

The world's #1 search engine. Handles ~85% of all searches. Ad-supported, extremely accurate algorithm.

Bing

Microsoft's search engine. Integrated into Windows and Edge. Offers AI-powered chat (Copilot).

DuckDuckGo

Privacy-focused search engine. Doesn't track you, doesn't store history. Great for private searches.

Search tip: Use quotes for exact phrases ("best pizza in Mumbai"). Use site: to search only within a site (site:egyan.in shortcuts). Use - to exclude words (jaguar -car).

4. Internet Services

The Internet delivers many different services — not just websites. Here are the essential ones every user should know:

World Wide Web

The "web" — billions of linked web pages. Accessed via browsers. Uses HTTP/HTTPS. The most visible part of the Internet.

Email

Electronic mail. Send messages + attachments worldwide in seconds. Protocols: SMTP, IMAP, POP3. Still the backbone of business communication.

Instant Messaging

WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal — real-time chat and voice/video calls. Uses end-to-end encryption in most modern apps.

Video Conferencing

Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams — face-to-face meetings online. Includes screen sharing, chat, and recording.

Cloud Storage

Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox — store files on remote servers. Accessible from any device. Auto-sync + version history.

Video Streaming

YouTube, Netflix, Prime Video, Hotstar — on-demand video over the Internet. Uses adaptive streaming based on your speed.

E-Commerce

Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra — buy and sell online. Includes payments, reviews, delivery tracking, and returns.

Online Banking

Banking, UPI, bill payment, fund transfer — all from your phone. Encrypted and secured with 2FA / OTP.

E-Learning

Online courses, video lectures, virtual classrooms. Coursera, Khan Academy, YouTube tutorials — education without borders.

Social Media

Facebook, Instagram, X (Twitter), LinkedIn — connect, share, and follow. Billions of posts per day.

FTP / File Transfer

Transfer large files between computers. Used by developers to upload websites. Modern alternative: SFTP (secure).

VoIP

Voice Over IP — phone calls over the Internet (WhatsApp calls, Skype). Cheaper than traditional telecom.

5. Online Safety & Security

The Internet is powerful, but it comes with real risks. Learn to protect yourself:

Strong Passwords

12+ characters mixing cases, numbers, and symbols. Never reuse passwords. Use a password manager. Enable 2FA wherever possible.

Phishing Attacks

Fake emails/messages tricking you into giving passwords or clicking bad links. Never click links from unknown senders. Check the sender's exact address.

HTTPS Everywhere

Only enter passwords or card details on HTTPS sites (look for the 🔒 lock). HTTP = not secure. Never ignore browser warnings.

Public Wi-Fi Risks

Coffee-shop Wi-Fi can be intercepted. Avoid banking on public Wi-Fi. Use a VPN. Or tether to your phone's hotspot instead.

Malware & Viruses

Only download from official sources. Keep antivirus updated. Don't open suspicious attachments. Avoid pirated software — it's a common infection route.

Privacy

Review app permissions. Use private/incognito mode for sensitive searches. Delete old accounts. Don't overshare personal details on social media.

Cookies & Trackers

Websites use cookies to remember you. Accept only necessary ones. Use a tracker blocker for extra privacy.

Report Abuse

If you see cyberbullying, fraud, or harassment — report it. In India, cybercrime.gov.in handles online crimes.

Never do this: Share your OTP with anyone. No bank, government, or company will ever ask for it. OTPs are the last line of defense for your money and accounts.
Golden rule: If it seems too good to be true — "You've won ₹1,00,000!" or "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" — it's almost certainly a scam. Slow down, verify, don't click.
