1. What is MS Paint?

Microsoft Paint (also called MS Paint or just Paint) is a simple raster graphics editor bundled with every version of Windows since Windows 1.0 (1985). It lets you create, edit, and save images in common formats like PNG, JPEG, BMP, and GIF.

Though not as powerful as Photoshop, Paint is free, fast, and beginner-friendly — perfect for quick diagrams, sketches, annotations, cropping screenshots, and resizing photos. It's the ideal first step for anyone learning digital drawing.

Fun fact: MS Paint originally began as "ZSoft PC Paintbrush" in 1982 and was licensed by Microsoft. Its icon-set inspired generations of digital artists!

2. Paint Tools Explained

MS Paint gives you about a dozen drawing tools. Each has a specific job — learning them is the fastest path to making your first good drawing.

Pencil

Freehand 1-pixel drawing. Great for outlines, details, and signature-style strokes.

Brush

Thicker freehand strokes with multiple shapes — round, square, spray, calligraphy. Softer than the pencil.

Fill with Color

Fills an enclosed area with solid color. Clicks inside shapes act like a "paint bucket".

Color Picker

Samples a color from any pixel on the canvas — perfect for matching exact shades.

Eraser

Removes paint and reveals the white canvas (or your second color). Adjustable thickness.

Text

Adds typed text. Control font, size, bold/italic/underline. Text sits in a resizable frame.

Magnifier

Zooms in at 100%, 200%, 400%, or 800% for pixel-perfect editing.

Line

Draws straight lines at any angle. Hold Shift for perfectly horizontal, vertical, or 45°.

Curve

Draws a curved line. Drag endpoints first, then pull the curve into shape.

Rectangle

Draws a rectangle. Hold Shift for a perfect square.

Ellipse

Draws an oval. Hold Shift for a perfect circle.

Triangle

Draws a triangle. Hold Shift for equilateral.

Polygon

Draws multi-sided shapes. Click to place each corner; double-click to finish.

Shapes (Win 10/11)

Built-in library: hearts, stars, arrows, callouts, lightning bolts, and more.

Select (Rectangular)

Selects a rectangle of the canvas to move, copy, or crop.

Free-Form Select

Selects any shape by drawing around it — great for cutting out irregular objects.

Resize & Skew

Stretch, shrink, or skew selected content by percentage or pixels. Found in the Home ribbon.

Rotate

Rotate the image or selected area 90° / 180° / 270°, or flip horizontally / vertically.

Brush vs Pencil: The Pencil always draws 1-pixel lines — crisp but slow. The Brush has many styles (round, square, spray) with variable thickness. Use Pencil for outlines and Brush for shading.

4. File Types in Paint

When you save a picture in Paint, you pick a file format. Each has its own strengths:

PNG (.png)

Best choice for most drawings. Lossless compression, supports transparency, small file size.

JPEG (.jpg / .jpeg)

For photos. Small size but lossy compression (quality is slightly reduced each save). No transparency.

BMP (.bmp)

Uncompressed. Huge file size, but perfect quality. Used rarely today — avoid unless required.

GIF (.gif)

Limited to 256 colors. Great for simple logos or (with other tools) short animations.

TIFF (.tif)

High-quality, professional format for printing and publishing. Large file size.

SVG (Win 11 only)

Vector format — scales infinitely without pixelation. Modern Paint can open and save SVG.

Watch out: Every time you re-save a JPEG, you lose a little quality. If you must save multiple times, use PNG instead.

5. Tips & Real-World Uses

Paint is far more useful than people realize. Here are some practical ways you'll use it daily:

Crop Screenshots

Paste a screenshot (Ctrl+V), use Select, then Crop — instant trimming.

Annotate with Arrows

Draw a red arrow pointing to the important part of a diagram or screenshot. Great for guides.

Resize Photos

Use Resize to shrink a large photo under 100 KB for email or upload.

Meme & Caption

Add bold text to any picture. Text tool supports transparent background for clean overlays.

Digital Signatures

Sign a document, save as PNG with transparent background. Works well in emails and PDFs.

Simple Diagrams

Flowcharts, mind-maps, and wireframes — all doable in Paint with shapes and text.

Cover Sensitive Info

Use the rectangle + fill tool to blank out phone numbers or account details in screenshots.

Pixel Art

Turn on Gridlines, use the pencil at 800% zoom — Paint is a surprisingly great pixel-art editor.

Practice plan: Day 1 — draw shapes and lines. Day 2 — color-fill a simple scene. Day 3 — annotate a screenshot. Day 4 — create a logo for yourself. In 4 days you'll be fluent.

6. Modern Paint (Windows 11)

In 2021 Microsoft released a fully rebuilt Paint for Windows 11 — modern, dark-mode-ready, and packed with new features:

Dark Mode

The whole interface auto-adapts to your system theme. Perfect for long editing sessions.

Layers

Stack drawings without overlapping — like a professional editor. Toggle visibility, reorder, merge.

AI Background Removal

One-click "Remove Background" — powered by AI. Instantly isolates subjects in photos.

Better Eraser

Pixel-perfect erase with adjustable size and shape. No more rough patches.

Improved Selection

Smoother free-form select with edge-snapping. Easier cutouts for logos and signatures.

Better Text Tool

Live preview, spacing control, and Google-Fonts-like availability via system fonts.

Try it: If you're on Windows 11, the new Paint is free from the Microsoft Store. Most people still think Paint is stuck in 2010 — they're missing out on layers and AI removal!

Whether you're using the classic Paint from Windows 10 or the modern Windows 11 version, the core skills you learn — tools, colors, shortcuts — stay the same. Master them once and they last forever.
